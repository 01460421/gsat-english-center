參考範文（355 字）

The picture captures the most thrilling moment of a school sports meet: the class relay race. On the track, a runner in a red shirt is stretching out his arm to pass the baton to his teammate, who is reaching back to grab it without slowing down. Just behind them, in the other lane, a runner in blue is pushing hard, hoping that a clumsy handoff will give him a chance to catch up. On the grass beside the track, three classmates are waving their flags high above their heads, cheering so loudly that the runners can surely hear them. Meanwhile, near the finish line, a teacher is holding up a stopwatch, ready to record the result. Every person in the scene tells the same story: in a relay, no runner is ever really running alone.

Last October, I ran the third leg of our class relay, and I still remember how heavy that light plastic baton felt in my hand. For weeks, our team had practiced handoffs during lunch breaks, because our PE teacher kept reminding us that relays are often won or lost in the passing zone. Even so, when my best friend raced toward me on the big day, my legs were trembling. I started running a moment too early, and for one terrible second, I thought I would miss the baton. Fortunately, she pushed it firmly into my palm, and I ran the fastest hundred meters of my life. We finished second, only half a second behind the winners, yet nobody on our team felt like a loser. Having both run in relays and cheered from the side, I much prefer being on the track, for two reasons. First, the pressure forces me to focus in a way that ordinary classes rarely do. Second, a relay teaches me that individual speed means little unless the baton is passed on smoothly to the next runner. Admittedly, cheering is less stressful, and loud supporters truly lift the runners' spirits. Nevertheless, I would rather carry the baton myself. In short, the fear of letting my teammates down is exactly what makes the race unforgettable.

第一段由跑道寫到場邊，交棒的動作可以用 pass the baton to …；第二段先用過去式寫經驗，再用 I would rather … than … 或 I prefer … to … 表明選擇。

參考範文（365 字）

The two pictures tell a before-and-after story of a school cafeteria. In the first picture, taken before the new rule, the trash can at the entrance is overflowing: its lid cannot close, and plastic spoons, foam bowls, and paper cups have spilled onto the floor, attracting a few flies. A student stands nearby with an empty lunch box in his hand, unsure where to throw it away. The second picture shows the same room after the rule took effect, and it looks calmer and cleaner. A sign on the wall announces that disposable tableware is no longer provided, so students line up with their own steel containers and chopsticks while the cafeteria staff fill the containers with rice and vegetables. Instead of a mountain of garbage, the entrance now has a row of sinks, where several students are rinsing their bowls, and a small trash can that remains almost empty.

I fully support the ban, and my own experience shows why. Before my school introduced the same rule last year, I used at least two sets of disposable chopsticks and a paper bowl every day, which added up to roughly four hundred sets in a single school year. I had never thought about that number until our homeroom teacher asked us to count. When the ban began, I complained along with everyone else. Carrying a steel lunch box made my backpack heavier, and washing it in a crowded line after lunch felt like a waste of break time. For example, on one rainy day I forgot my chopsticks and had to eat fried rice with a borrowed spoon, much to my classmates’ amusement. Within a month, though, the routine became automatic, and the change was visible: the cleaning team on our floor now takes out one bag of trash a day instead of three. Admittedly, the rule can be inconvenient for students who buy lunch at the last minute, so the cafeteria could sell low-cost reusable sets. Still, the inconvenience is temporary, while the habit lasts. In the end, a rule that seemed strict at first has taught me that protecting the environment begins with what we carry in our own bags.

比較段可抓三組對比：垃圾桶、學生手上的餐具、門口的設備；第二段可以用「一開始不方便 → 後來習慣 → 看到改變」的時間順序。

參考範文（339 字）

The two pictures capture how ordering at a fast-food restaurant has changed in recent years. In the first scene, five customers are lined up at the counter, where two staff members are busy taking orders and handing a loaded tray to the customer at the front. The process looks slow but personal, since every customer gets to speak to a real person. The second scene, by contrast, is dominated by three tall self-ordering kiosks. Two young customers are tapping through the menu in seconds, while only one worker remains behind the counter to call out order numbers. Yet the most striking figure stands at the last machine: an elderly man holding his reading glasses, frowning at the screen as if it were written in a foreign language. Behind him, the line keeps growing, making him look even more overwhelmed.

Personally, I welcome the kiosks, but I do not think they should replace cashiers entirely. Admittedly, some degree of automation is inevitable, and the machines bring clear benefits: they cut waiting time, reduce mistakes in orders, and help restaurants save on labor costs. However, efficiency should not come at the expense of the customers who are left behind, especially older people who find touch screens intimidating. My grandfather is a case in point. Last summer, he tried to buy breakfast at a fast-food restaurant on his own, only to find that the counter had no cashier at all. After pressing the wrong button three times, he gave up and went home hungry. When he told me about it, he laughed, yet I could hear the embarrassment in his voice. What troubled me most was not the machine itself but the message it seemed to send: customers like him no longer belonged there. Therefore, I believe every restaurant should keep at least one staffed counter or assign a worker to guide customers at the kiosks during busy hours. After all, hospitality is not only about speed; a good restaurant should make every customer, young or old, feel welcome.

先列出兩張圖的三組對比（誰服務、速度、誰覺得困難），第二段的立場要和經驗互相呼應，不要經驗講方便、結論卻說反對。

    The survey reveals a clear shift in reading habits as students grow older. Among junior high students, paper books are clearly dominant, chosen by 62% of readers, while e-books and audiobooks account for only 25% and 13% respectively. As students move up the educational ladder, however, the picture changes steadily. By senior high school, paper has slipped to 45%, and e-books have climbed to 38%, nearly catching up. At the college level, the order is reversed: e-books lead with 44%, whereas paper falls to 30%. Audiobooks, though still the least popular format in every group, show the fastest relative growth, doubling from 13% to 26%. Taken together, the figures suggest that the older students become, the further they move away from the printed page.

    In my opinion, each format serves a different purpose rather than replacing the others. Printed books are ideal for deep reading; when I prepared for a history report last spring, I found that underlining a paper copy helped me remember details far better than scrolling through a screen. Their weaknesses, however, are just as real: they are heavy to carry around and usually cost more than digital editions. E-books, on the other hand, are portable and affordable, but the same tablet that holds a novel also delivers notifications, which can easily break one's concentration. Audiobooks turn wasted time, such as a crowded bus ride, into reading time, yet listeners may miss key points because they cannot glance back at a page. Since no single format wins in every situation, I believe the ideal approach for high school students is a flexible mix. Textbooks and challenging works deserve paper, light fiction suits e-readers, and audiobooks can fill the minutes spent commuting or exercising. Some may argue that switching formats is confusing, but choosing a tool according to the task is exactly what skilled readers do. In the end, the format matters less than the habit. What we should protect is the willingness to keep reading, whatever form the words take.

    第二段可以用「各有用途」的架構：每種形式寫一個優點、一個缺點，最後建議依讀書目的搭配使用，讓建議直接從評估推出來。
